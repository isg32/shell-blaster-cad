# Shell-ejecting soft-dart toy blaster: build guide

This is an original, minimal-style, spring-and-plunger **toy**. You rack the slide, the empty shell
flips out to the right, the next shell loads, and the trigger fires a soft-tip dart with a puff of air.

> **Toy-safety rules.** Only use bought **soft-tip dart refills** (or soft TPU later). Never print
> hard projectiles. Keep the main spring light (indoor-safe). The orange muzzle stays orange:
> never paint it or leave it off.

Everything is generated by **`blaster.FCMacro`**. Edit the parameters at the top and re-run it.
`blaster.FCStd` is the saved assembly (slide forward), and `stl/` holds the print-ready parts.

```
freecadcmd blaster.FCMacro            # headless build
# or in the FreeCAD GUI: Macro > Macros... > blaster.FCMacro > Execute
# set EXPORT_STL = True to (re)write stl/*.stl, laid flat for printing
```

**Extended mag:** holds 8 shells and sticks out ~70 mm below the front handle (`MAG_CAPACITY`).

**Layout (v3):** a raked pistol grip at the rear holds the trigger linkage and the catch. The magazine
well in front doubles as a front handle, and a thick trigger guard runs from the grip into the handle. The
black slide runs the full length (side groove line, oval racking pads at the rear). When you rack it, it
sticks out 32 mm at the back. Orange accents: the curved trigger, the mag base and the full-face muzzle cap.

Overall size is about 246 × 34 × 122 mm. The longest printed parts are the frame halves (246 mm) and the
slide (235 mm), which fit the Centauri Carbon's 256 × 256 bed.

---

## 1. First test print: shell + magazine + one dart

Print these first, before the big parts: `Shell_Chambered` ×2, `Mag_Body`, `Mag_Follower`, `Mag_Base`.

- Push a dart into a shell. It should grip snugly and not fall out when shaken.
  - Too loose: make `DART_FIT` more negative (e.g. −0.2).
  - Too tight: use −0.05.
  - **Measure your darts** and set `DART_D` / `DART_L` first. Every bore is derived from them.
- Load 8 shells into the mag (rim to the rear) with the spring and follower. The top shell should sit
  flat under both lips, with its rim held by the lips, and slide forward out of the front freely.
- Push the top shell down 3–4 mm with a finger and release. It must pop back up under the lips.

## 4. Assembly order

1. **Plunger:** press the rod's front end into the head (add a drop of CA). Fit the O-ring and
   lightly grease it with silicone grease.
2. Drop the plunger into the **Bolt** tube, head first, then add the **main spring**.
3. **TubeCap:** pass the rod through the cap. Push the cap in with its lugs lined up with the two
   slots, then turn it a quarter turn to lock.
4. **Extractor:** press its base into the pocket on the nozzle rib (hook at the front, pointing
   down) and add a drop of CA.
5. Lay the bolt into the **Slide** from below (the boss fits its pocket) and fix it with the M3×6 from the top.
6. **Frame_R (right half), lying split face up:**
   - Catch: put the spring in its pocket, then the lever (foot pointing down and outward), then the Ø3 pin.
   - Trigger: peg and glue `Trigger_Bar` onto `Trigger`. Put the return spring in the trigger
     window, in front of the frame post, and lay the assembly in its channels. The bar's rear end at the
     rear must sit just in front of the catch's foot. The catch lives in the frame's rear block.
7. Slide the slide assembly onto the rails from the front, then close with **Frame_L**. Fit the 7 nuts
   and screws.
   - Mag release: drop the small spring into the end hole of `Mag_Release` and lay the latch in its channel
     behind the magwell, with the button out through the right side.
8. Press and glue the **Muzzle_Orange** cap onto the barrel nose (its socket is 7.7 mm deep).
9. Load the mag and push it up into the front handle until the latch clicks under the tab on the mag's back. Press the orange button to drop the mag.

**Operation:** hold the rear grip and the front handle. Rack the slide fully back and push it forward, then pull the trigger. Racking again
extracts and ejects the empty shell to the right and chambers the next one.

## 5. Tuning

All values are parameters at the top of `blaster.FCMacro`.

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| Air leaks, weak shot | Seal: tighten the O-ring fit (a bigger `ORING_ID` makes the groove shallower) or use a thicker O-ring |
| Plunger very stiff | The O-ring is too tight; go the other way, and add grease |
| Too strong for indoors | Use a lighter main spring (keep the wire ≤ 1.0 mm) or shorten `STROKE`. Never go stronger |
| Main spring coil-binds | `SPRING_SPACE − STROKE` must be ≥ the spring's solid length (the macro asserts this) |
| Extractor won't snap over the rim | Thinner arm (`EXTRACTOR_T` 1.0) or print the extractor in a softer PETG |
| Extractor loses the shell | Thicker arm (`EXTRACTOR_T` 1.4) |
| Shell drops before ejecting, or ejects too early | Ejector timing: `EJECT_REMAIN` (slide travel left when the rim hits the post, default 4 mm) |
| Shell won't leave the mag top | Check the lip gap (0.7 × `RIM_D`) and use a weaker mag spring |
| Top shell rides too low or too high | `MAG_LIP_T` / the shell rim size (`RIM_D` = shell OD + 3) |
| Dart falls out or sticks in the shell | `DART_FIT` |
| Catch won't release | Reduce `FOOT_GAP` so the trigger bar pushes the catch foot further |
| Catch slips when cocked | Deepen `NOTCH_DEPTH` a little (the rod core stays ≥ 3.6 mm) |

## 6. Design checks the macro runs

`run_tests()`, `mech_tests()` and `cycle_sim()` can be called after loading the macro.

- Every part is a valid single solid.
- **Zero unintended overlap** between all part pairs, with the slide forward *and* back. The press fits
  (dart in shell, rod in head, extractor in rib, bar peg) are the only whitelisted pairs.
- **Extraction:** the chambered shell, dragged back by the extractor, passes the frame, mag and slide.
- **Ejector:** the rim meets the ejector post with `EJECT_REMAIN` (4 mm) of slide travel left.
- **Ejection:** the shell swings out of the right-side port up to 80° yaw, clear of everything.
  (The port-side lip is raised above the shell body and hung from a top bridge for this.)
- **Feed:** the next shell slides from the mag into the chamber without hitting anything.
- **Catch / trigger:**
  - At rest the trigger bar doesn't touch the lever, so the rod tail can push it down while you cock.
  - The catch holds the rod against the spring.
  - Pulling the trigger pushes the catch foot back, rotating the lever ~17° and dropping the tooth below the rod.
  - The lever can swing that far inside the frame.
- **Print:** every part lies flat at Z = 0 with real bed contact, fits on a 256 mm bed, and exports
  as a closed STL.

The ejection check is a kinematic sweep. Real ejection also depends on spring speed, so expect to fine-tune
`EJECT_REMAIN` and the extractor after the first assembled test.

### Full-cycle simulation (`cycle_sim()`)
Steps the mechanism through a whole shot and checks every moving part against everything else at each step:
1. Rack back in 4 mm steps, with 0.5 mm steps while the rod tail rides over the catch. The rod pushes the
   catch down ~9°, and the pushed catch is clear of the frame and trigger bar.
2. At full back the catch drops into the notch and holds the rod.
3. Push forward with the plunger held. The spring compresses to 19 mm, above its 18 mm solid length.
4. Pull the trigger in 0.5 mm steps. The plunger is released at 4 mm of trigger travel.
5. Fire. The plunger runs the full stroke clear of the dropped catch. The plunger moves ~10 000 mm³ of
   air, more than the volume of the shell and barrel ahead of the dart (~8 400 mm³).

### Mag release checks (in `mech_tests()`)
- The latch holds the mag up: the mag's rear tab rests on the latch.
- The button moves its full 3.5 mm travel without touching the frame.
- With the button pressed, the mag drops all the way out without hitting the latch.
- Pushing a mag in, the tab rides the ramp and pushes the latch in by up to 3.25 mm (less than the 3.5 mm
  of travel). The latch springs back under the tab once the mag is seated.
